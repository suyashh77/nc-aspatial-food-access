What this is

We're turning Google reviews into food-access scores for every grocery store in North Carolina. A model sorts each review into the five dimensions of access below. Your labels check whether it does that correctly. There are two tasks. You can do them in either order, stop at any time and come back. Every answer saves to your account as you go.

You won't see other raters' labels, and they won't see yours. Comparing independent labels is how we measure agreement.

Task 1 · Label reviews (260 reviews, each labeled by 5 people)

Read one review at a time. For each dimension, say whether the review talks about it, and whether positively or negatively. These labels are the answer key every method gets graded against.

The reviews are shared out among everyone. The page gives you the next review that still needs raters. Label as many as you can (about 30 seconds each). When every review has 5 raters, the page tells you you're done.

Task 2 · Check topics (83 topics in about 25 groups, about 1.5–2 hours)

The model grouped all 975,000 reviews into 83 topics. For each group of similar topics, read the keywords and example reviews. Mark topics that are too vague to use, move any that are in the wrong group, then score the group 0–3 on each dimension. This decides which reviews count toward store scores.

Task 3 · Check sentence topics (optional; 83 topics in 30 groups, about 1.5 hours)

The improved pipeline splits reviews into sentences and groups those into their own 83 topics. Checking them works exactly like Task 2. Switch to "Sentence topics" at the top of the Check topics page.

The five dimensions

Judgement calls (so raters agree)

  • Selection, variety, "has everything" → Availability
  • Checkout lines, registers, pickup, delivery, layout, finding items → Accommodation
  • Rude, friendly or helpful staff; clean or dirty; fresh or rotten → Acceptability
  • "Close to home", parking, getting in → Accessibility. "Wish there was one near me" → Availability
  • "Great store!" talks about no dimension at all.

Check topics

How to check a group (open for the steps)
  1. Pick a group on the left. Groups put near-duplicate topics together, so you score them once.
  2. For each topic, read the keywords and the example reviews. "Most typical" reviews sit at the topic's centre. "Random" reviews show its range.
  3. Mark a topic invalid if it has no clear subject (pure praise, place names, brand names) and give a reason. Groups called "General praise" or "No clear subject" start as invalid. Change one to valid only if its examples show a real subject. Look hardest at Topic 0 (58,000 reviews about "the grocery store"): its label is generic, but many of its reviews talk about selection, which is Availability.
  4. If a topic belongs in another group, move it there. Its new group will need checking again.
  5. Score the group 0–3 on each dimension separately. These are not ranks, and two dimensions can both score 3. Judge what the reviews are about, not whether they're positive: "prices are crazy" and "great deals" both score 3 on Affordability.
  6. Save. The next unchecked group opens.
