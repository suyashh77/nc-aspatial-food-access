THE DECISION I NEED FROM YOU

Should the end-of-November deliverable be only the replication of Teresa's method, or should I also run a small sentence-level pilot to test whether her one-topic-per-review design changes store scores?

My recommendation: finish the replication first, and run the pilot as a clearly separate extension. No option I tested fixes the problem without leaving review-level labels.

How a review becomes a score

Teresa's method gives each review one topic. The topic decides which of the five access dimensions the review counts toward, and the star rating decides whether it counts as good or bad.

  1. Review975,388 NC reviews with text, from 4,383 stores
  2. One topicBERTopic gives each review a single topic. This is the step in question.
  3. DimensionMatched per topic, then checked by hand. Not run yet for NC.
  4. Good or bad1 star = −1, 2–4 stars = 0, 5 stars = +1
  5. Store scorePartial pooling, then z-scores, then tract analysis

The scale-up itself looks sound. At 83 topics, coherence matches Raleigh (0.529 vs 0.528) and the first-pass outlier share is close (57% vs 52%). The concern is what any model of this design can represent.

The problem

BERTopic groups reviews by how they are written, which doesn't always line up with what the reviewer is evaluating. Our scores need the second.

A REAL NC REVIEW

Clean and not crowded. Staff are always friendly and helpful. Prices are a little high on most things but you can find some deals. ★★★★☆ · scores 0

TODAY: WHOLE REVIEW

  • One topic: staff, clean

The comment on prices has no route to Affordability unless that topic is labelled with it. Four stars scores 0 on whatever it counts toward.

EACH SENTENCE ON ITS OWN

  • Sentence 1 → clean
  • Sentence 2 → staff, helpful
  • Sentence 3 → prices, deals

Each subject is now visible. The single star rating still can't say which one was good or bad.

Several subjects, one topic

45% of reviews have two or more sentences. In a sample of 20,000 of them, 79% had sentences closest to two or more different non-generic topics.

"Good prices. Clean. Friendly service." went to a topic labelled service, clean.

Passing mentions get lost

Of 16,671 reviews that mention parking, 47% land in the parking topic at first pass and 37% are outliers. Mentioning parking doesn't always mean the review is about it, so this is an upper limit on the loss.

Mixed opinions flatten to zero

13% of reviews contain contrast words such as "but". 51% of those give 2–4 stars and score 0, against 31% of all reviews.

"Good selection but prices are high" gave 3 stars, so it scores 0 for both.

One subject, split by writing style

Rude and friendly cashiers are separate topics (1.5 vs 4.6 stars). Their median lengths are 45 and 11 words, so long complaints and short praise may be separating as much as the opinion is. I haven't isolated that effect.

What the problem is not

The usual explanation is that adjectives fragment a subject into "great parking", "easy parking" and "terrible parking". That doesn't happen here. There is one parking topic, it holds 94% of the 1,578 short parking reviews, and its ratings run from 18% 1-star to 33% 5-star. The model also places "the parking lot is great" much closer to "...is terrible" (0.81) than to "the produce is great" (0.33). So I have focused on reviews that cover several subjects.

Automatic topic merging

REJECTED
PUSHBACK
Why group by hand when BERTopic has a hierarchy built in?
TESTED
Cut BERTopic's method (Ward linkage on topic word weights) to 25 groups.
RESULT
Parking landed in one group with deli, order pickup, rude staff, closed, pharmacy, furniture, Target, star ratings, expired items and tires. That group crosses dimensions, so the hierarchy can only suggest groups for a person to approve.

Strip sentiment words before embedding

REJECTED
PUSHBACK
Would removing opinion words stop subjects splitting by sentiment?
TESTED
Removed generic and opinion words from 3,000 reviews each in the rude and friendly cashier topics, re-embedded them and compared the topic centres.
RESULT
Similarity barely moves (0.70 to 0.74). It would add another departure for little gain, and a broader word list could remove meaning such as "rotten" or "fresh".

What I propose

Two separate tracks, so the extension can't blur the replication.

TRACK A · REPLICATION

Teresa's method at NC scale

  1. Keep the 83-topic model, with praise words hidden from labels only.
  2. Hand-approve parent groups, keeping the original topics.
  3. Map to dimensions by hand and with her automatic matching, then compare.
  4. Score stores with stars, then tract scores and regressions.
  5. Check against Raleigh on the 42 shared stores.
TRACK B · EXTENSION

Sentence-level pilot

  1. Split reviews into sentences and assign each to a parent group.
  2. Hand-label about 300 for dimension and for good or bad.
  3. Compare review-level and sentence-level accuracy on those labels.
  4. Rescore stores and compare rankings with Track A, per dimension.

My rough estimate is about a week. It starts only once Track A is safe.

Questions for you

1

Is the replication enough for this deliverable, or should the pilot be in scope?

I recommend including it as a separate extension. If the deadline tightens, it is the first thing to drop.

2

How much would rankings need to change for you to adopt sentence-level scores?

The pilot can't settle anything without a rule set in advance. As a starting point, I'd use the plan's replication bar: consider adopting if sentence labels are more accurate and the Spearman correlation with Track A falls below 0.8 on any dimension.

3

Are hand-approved parent groups acceptable as a method step?

They add human judgment that Teresa's topic-level check didn't have at this level. I would report them as a departure and keep both levels so the results can be compared.

What these tests can't show

  • No hand labels yet. The tests show where reviews land. They don't show that the placement is correct.
  • No dimension scores yet. Matching hasn't been run for NC, so I can't yet measure the effect of any option on final store scores. The 0.996 figure covers overall star signal only.
  • Word lists are rough. The parking, price, contrast and Acceptability-type figures rely on simple keyword matches. Treat them as sizes, not precise rates.
  • A known issue I'd leave in place. Her cleaning removes "lion" from "Food Lion", leaving "food". Two "food" topics are 29% and 21% Food Lion reviews, against 3.1% overall. Fixing it means refitting everything, and the same thing happens in her method, so I'd report it unless you want it fixed.
  • Who reviews. Reviewers are self-selected, so all of this measures perceived access as reported online. Her thesis states this limit too.
